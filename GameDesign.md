# 2. Gameplay Core

- **Core Gameplay Loop:**  
  Explore dungeon -> die horribly -> respawn -> maybe survive this time → rinse and repeat. Learning is the real loot here.  

- **Primary Mechanics:**  
  Move around, dodge stuff, maybe fight a little(cause I'm bad with animation😩🤌), poke things, push buttons, look cool in your 2D sprite outfit.

- **Secondary Systems:**  
  Pick some upgrades, choose a path, touch some futuristic gadgets, maybe even press a hologram button (or fail spectacularly).

- **Progression Systems:**  
  You level up in **knowledge**, not stats. Dungeon = brain gym.

  68% of indie devs (GDC said it) **actually use flowcharts** before coding. So if this goes sideways, blame the flowchart.

# 3. Narrative and Characters

- **Worldbuilding:**  
  A cursed dungeon that is basically **medieval on the outside, Star Wars on the inside**. Holograms, weird tech, and very dumb traditions all in one place.

- **Plot Outline:**  
  You are stuck. You die. You respawn. You try again. The dungeon doesn’t care. Repeat. It’s kinda sad, kinda hilarious.

- **Character Bios:**  
  Mostly you, the protagonist. And some mysterious NPCs who may or may not be helpful. Or evil. Or both.  

- **Narrative Mechanics:**  
  Story happens through the environment, little interactions, and repeated runs. Dialogue? Optional. Drama? Mandatory.

- **Personal Note:**  
  First team project lesson: don’t skip narrative mechanics. Otherwise, **UI redesign hell** incoming.

# 4. Art and Aesthetics

- **Visual Style Guide:**  
  2D sprite characters in a **low-poly 3D PS1-style world**. Retro, crunchy, and pixel-y in all the right ways.

- **Mood Boards:**  
  Dark, eerie, mysterious… but still funny at times. Futuristic-meets-medieval mashup.

- **Accessibility Considerations:**  
  Clear shapes, high contrast, readable fonts. Don’t make people squint while dying for the 10th time.
